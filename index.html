The super manager

Payday super, counted to the day it is due

Contributions have to reach a fund within seven business days of the fee being paid, and twenty for the first one you ever make for a person. GigFee counts those days the way the ATO counts them, keeps a ledger of who is owed what, and stamps every invoice with the rate for its own financial year.

  • Business days, properly counted. Weekends and national public holidays both push the date out, so a gig played at Christmas does not quietly fall due on Boxing Day.
  • Twenty days for a person's first contribution, seven after that, tracked per person rather than per invoice.
  • Last year's rate on last year's invoices. The rate comes from the invoice date, so reopening an old one does not rewrite what a client was sent.
  • On top of the fee, or inside it. Both are real arrangements, and the app does the arithmetic either way.
  • A door deal or a private household owes none. Mark it and no figure is produced, whatever the rate says.
  • A ledger, not a reminder. Every contribution, what has been paid, what is late, and which payment covered it.

The band

Split a fee without putting it on the invoice

Divide a night's money between the band equally, by percentage, or by set amounts. The client is still billed once for the full fee, and none of the split appears on the copy they receive.

GigFee tracks who you still owe, so money that only passed through your account on its way to someone else is not counted as your income. Each member's super is worked out against their own share, with their fund details kept alongside it.

Money in, money out

What you spent, with the receipt attached to it

A deduction you cannot prove is a deduction you do not get. GigFee files the receipt at the moment you buy the thing, which is the only moment you still have it.

  • Receipts, filed on the spot

    Photograph it, or attach the PDF. The file is copied into the app, so it still opens at tax time even if the original is long gone, and it goes into your backup with everything else.

  • Categorised for the return

    Gear, strings, rehearsal rooms, fuel, travel, phone, software, promotion, lessons, insurance, stage clothing, wear and tear. Added up by category in Reports and in your spreadsheet export.

  • Cash recorded as cash

    Say how an invoice was paid and see the year's cash income as its own figure. Cash income is income, and every report here counts it as such.

  • Off the books, on the record

    Mark a gig off the books and GigFee stops asking you for an invoice. No GST is charged and no super worked out, the fee stays out of the tax figures, and it is still reported under its own heading so nothing goes missing.

  • The diary carries through

    Bring bookings in from your phone's calendar, see what is ahead, and find the gigs you played but never invoiced. Projected earnings for the month or the financial year.

  • A spreadsheet your accountant can use

    Invoices issued, bills you were sent and expenses you recorded, a table each with the GST broken out, in CSV or Excel. With every invoice PDF in the same archive if you want them.

The figures you carry to your accountant

GST collected and GST paid, super shown against the gigs that attracted it, deductions by category, and the travel claim worked out at the rate each trip was entered at. By financial year, by quarter, or across the lot.

Whether a particular amount is business income, or a particular purchase deductible, is your call and your accountant's. GigFee keeps the record and does the arithmetic. It does not make that judgement for you, and it says so where that matters.

Privacy

Your books stay yours

Your invoices carry your clients' details and everything you earn. That is not something to hand over lightly, so GigFee is built not to ask for it.

No account
Install it and start invoicing. There is no sign-up, and you can use GigFee for years without ever telling us who you are.
No tracking
No analytics, no advertising, no third party trackers. Nothing in here watches how you use it.
Backup is yours to switch on
Cloud backup stays off until you turn it on. When you do, the copy is stored in Australia and readable only by the account you signed in with. Your phone also keeps its own copy through Android's backup, which is covered in the policy.
